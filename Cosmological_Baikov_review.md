# Review of “Cosmological Baikov” and Mathematica implementation

## Bottom line

The central mechanism is sound for the **pinched (I^{110}) subfamily**:

1. differentiate with respect to (a), which raises the power of (Lambda) and inserts (D_6);
2. use the scaling IBP to remove the extra power of (Lambda);
3. eliminate (D_6) algebraically;
4. use ordinary/tangential IBPs and Hermite reduction to close on five masters.

The five integrals in equation (11), however, are **not a master basis for the whole family in equation (1)**. They form a generic basis for

\[
\bar I^{us0,v00;0}
\]

and hence for the (I^{110}) pinch used in equation (6). The full (I^{111}) equation also requires the corresponding reductions of the (I^{101}) and (I^{011}) pinches. The (I^{101}) system follows by (2\leftrightarrow3), (a\leftrightarrow b), (x_2^2\leftrightarrow x_3^2). The (I^{011}) pinch is a distinct two-denominator sector and needs its analogous basis/reduction. Thus equation (6) is a correct bridge from the top sector to its pinches, but equation (11) alone does not reduce every integral in (1).

The accompanying `CosmologicalBaikov.wl` implements and tests the (I^{110}) system, including all entries omitted as (A_{41},\ldots,A_{55}).

## Confirmed steps

Writing (X_0=x_0^2), (X_2=x_2^2), (X_3=x_3^2),

\[
\lambda_0=\frac{c^2}{4}-ab,
\qquad
\Lambda=c\,p_2\!\cdot p_3-bp_2^2-ap_3^2+\lambda_0.
\]

The decomposition needed for equation (6) is

\[
\Lambda=-\frac c2D_1+\left(\frac c2-b\right)D_2
+\left(\frac c2-a\right)D_3+\widetilde\lambda_0,
\]

with

\[
\widetilde\lambda_0
=\lambda_0+bX_2+aX_3+\frac c2(X_0-X_2-X_3).
\]

Multiplying \(\partial_a\bar I^{111}\) by this identity gives equation (6) exactly.

Equation (9) follows from the scaling IBP generated by

\[
p_2\!\cdot\partial_{p_2}+p_3\!\cdot\partial_{p_3}
\]

in (d=1-2\epsilon). Equation (10) then follows by replacing (t\to t-1) in (9). These signs and coefficients check out.

The field in equation (12) is also correct. It can be written as

\[
T=(\partial_{p_3}\Lambda)\!\cdot\partial_{p_2}
 -(\partial_{p_2}\Lambda)\!\cdot\partial_{p_3},
\]

so (T\Lambda=0), and its divergence vanishes. The test suite verifies both statements algebraically.

## Corrections and missing definitions

### 1. Subsection title

Section 1.1 should be titled “Differential equation for (I^{110}),” not (I^{011}). Its parametrization (\bar I^{us0,v0t;j}) and the sentence before equation (22) both refer to the (I^{110}) pinch.

### 2. Equation (7)

The first term in the second line must be

\[
\frac{c^2}{4}-ab,
\]

not (c^2/2-ab). Otherwise it disagrees with equations (3), (5), and the following occurrence of \(\lambda_0\).

### 3. Equation (8)

Provided the regulated measure is independent of (a), the proportionality sign can be replaced by equality (up to whatever overall normalization is included in the definition of the measure):

\[
\partial_a\bar I^{us0,v0t;j}
=\left(j+\frac12\right)\bar I^{us0,v0,t-1;j+1}.
\]

### 4. Equation (13)

The symbols \(\gamma_{us}\) and (d) are not defined. As written, (13) is therefore not executable. The implementation avoids this ambiguity by deriving the needed relations directly from the four vector IBPs and the scaling identity.

### 5. Equation (21): one-denominator reductions

Let

\[
h=a+b-c.
\]

The reduction of (\bar I^{200,0}) is

\[
\bar I^{200,0}=r_0\bar I^{000,0}+r_1\bar I^{100,0},
\]

with

\[
r_0=\frac{4\epsilon-1}{2X_0(h-X_0)},\qquad
r_1=\frac{(1+2\epsilon)h-(1+4\epsilon)X_0}
{2X_0(h-X_0)}.
\]

Thus the factor in the manuscript’s (r_1) numerator should be (1+4\epsilon), not (1-4\epsilon).

The displayed formula for (\bar I^{020,-1}) is also inconsistent with the IBPs. The corrected relation is particularly simple:

\[
\bar I^{020,-1}=\frac{c}{2a}
\left[\bar I^{010,0}+(a-X_2)\bar I^{020,0}\right],
\]

or, after inserting the (\bar I^{020,0}) reduction,

\[
\bar I^{020,-1}
=\frac{c(4\epsilon-1)}{4aX_2}\bar I^{000,0}
+\frac{c\,[a+X_2+2\epsilon(a-2X_2)]}{4aX_2}\bar I^{010,0}.
\]

The matrices \(\mathcal K\) and \(\mathcal B_\epsilon\) in (21) are not defined in the manuscript. The package constructs their equivalent directly: four IBPs at ((u,s,v)=(1,1,0)) give a (4\times4) linear system for

\[
(I^{210,-1},I^{210,0},I^{120,0},I^{120,-1}),
\]

then three independent IBPs at ((1,1,-1)) reduce the remaining (D_4^2) numerator and the two raised-index integrals needed for the last master.

### 6. Equation (23), third row

The coefficients (r_0,r_1) reduce (I^{200}); they are not the coefficients of \(\partial_a I^{100}\). With (h=a+b-c), the correct third row is

\[
A_{31}=\frac{4\epsilon-1}{2h(h-X_0)},\qquad
A_{33}=\frac{b\epsilon}{\lambda_0}
+\frac{4\epsilon-1}{2h}-\frac{\epsilon}{h-X_0},
\]

with the other entries in that row equal to zero.

The first two rows of (23) are correct. The package supplies the exact rational fourth and fifth rows through `DifferentialMatrixA[]`.

## Leading-singularity basis for the last two masters

On the maximal cut (D_1=D_2=0), write (z=D_4). The internal Gram/Baikov
polynomial becomes

\[
G_{\rm cut}(z)=X_0X_2-\left(z-\frac c2+X_2\right)^2,
\]

so its two branches are

\[
z_\pm=\frac c2-X_2\pm\sqrt{X_0X_2}.
\]

Since (I^{110,-100;0}) contains one (D_4) numerator relative to
(I^{110,000;0}), their leading singularities obey

\[
\frac{\operatorname{LS}_\pm(I^{110,-100;0})}
{\operatorname{LS}_\pm(I^{110,000;0})}=z_\pm.
\]

Thus the branch-adapted combinations are

\[
N_+=I^{110,-100;0}-z_+ I^{110,000;0},\qquad
N_-=I^{110,-100;0}-z_- I^{110,000;0}.
\]

They diagonalize the homogeneous (2\times2) top-sector block of (A) at
\(\epsilon=0\). At nonzero \(\epsilon\), both off-diagonal entries in this
basis are generally nonzero but proportional to \(\epsilon\). Therefore the
leading-singularity change of basis by itself does **not** give an exactly
triangular finite-\(\epsilon\) connection.

For exact triangularity, take (N=M_5+\rho(a)M_4) and order the top-sector
pair as \(\{N,M_4\}\), where (M_4=I^{110,000;0}). If the top block is
(B=(B_{ij})_{i,j=4,5}), the required coefficient satisfies

\[
\rho'=B_{45}\rho^2+(B_{55}-B_{44})\rho-B_{54}.
\]

This Riccati equation is precisely the contribution of the gauge term in

\[
A_{\rm new}=(\partial_aT)T^{-1}+TAT^{-1}.
\]

The leading singularities select the two branches through
(\rho(a,0)=-z_+\) or \(-z_-\). The accompanying notebook implements both the
algebraic \(\epsilon=0\) basis and this exact finite-\(\epsilon\) construction.

## What the code does

The package uses the notation

\[
\texttt{CBI[u,s,v]}=\bar I^{us0,v00;0}.
\]

Internally it:

1. represents (p_2^2,p_3^2,p_2\!\cdot p_3) by (D_1,D_2,D_4);
2. generates the four vector IBPs `pp`, `pq`, `qp`, `qq`;
3. removes every extra (1/\Lambda) with equation (9);
4. uses
   \[
   D_6=D_1-D_2+2D_4-(X_0-X_2-b+c);
   \]
5. performs the one-denominator Hermite reductions;
6. solves the two small mixed-sector systems;
7. assembles the exact matrix in \(\partial_a\mathbf m=A\mathbf m\).

Minimal Mathematica usage:

```wl
Get["/path/to/CosmologicalBaikov.wl"];

m = MasterBasis[];
A = DifferentialMatrixA[];                    (* fast, exact, unsimplified *)
As = DifferentialMatrixA["SimplifyFunction" -> Together];

TopSectorIdentity[]
ReductionRules[]
```

The entrywise `Together` form can take noticeably longer because the last two rows have large common denominators. `DifferentialMatrixA[]` already consists of exact rational expressions and is usually preferable for subsequent substitutions or numerical evaluation.

Run the tests with Wolfram 15:

```bash
/Applications/Wolfram.app/Contents/MacOS/WolframKernel \
  -noprompt -script RunCosmologicalBaikovTests.wl
```

## Genericity and boundary assumptions

The reduction is generic. At minimum it assumes

\[
\lambda_0\ne0,\quad a\ne0,\quad h\ne0,\quad
X_0X_2\ne0,\quad a-X_2\ne0,\quad h-X_0\ne0,
\]

and nonvanishing determinants of the two small mixed-sector IBP matrices. On any of these loci the displayed basis may degenerate and should be recomputed rather than obtained by direct substitution into formulas containing a zero denominator.

The IBPs also assume dimensional regularization/analytic continuation is sufficient to discard total derivatives. If a physical contour has endpoint or branch-cut contributions, those must be checked separately; algebraic IBP closure alone does not prove their absence.
